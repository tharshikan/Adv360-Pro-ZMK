Sunaku & the Command Layers

The board at rest, and the two maps your thumbs reach for without leaving home. Hold the right thumb's space bar and every key becomes a chord. Hold the left thumb's backspace instead and you get a different one. This page draws all three.

One base, two thumbs

Layer 0 is where typing happens — the sunaku alphabet with mods on the home row. Neither command layer has a key of its own: each is held by a thumb key that still types its own legend on a tap. That is the whole trick. Space and backspace keep working, and holding either one swaps the board under your fingers for as long as you keep it down.

hold left thumb ⌫
command left
cmd_arw_nav_left_hand · layer 8

Edit verbs under the left home row, raw arrow keys under the right. The layer you hold while rewriting a line.

hold right thumb ␣
command right
cmd_arw_nav_right_hand · layer 7

⌘ chords across the left hand, Meh chords across the right. The layer you hold while driving an app.

The node names read backwards at first glance: …_right_hand is the one the right thumb holds, not the one that remaps the right hand. Both carry the display name Nav, so the on-board layer readout cannot tell them apart.

What the home row costs you

Every mod on the home row is a tap-preferred hold-tap with retro-tap, so a key that times out still emits its letter rather than swallowing it. Two guards keep rolls from turning into chords: a positional one, and an idle one.

a hold only counts if the next key is on the other hand or a thumb

That is hold-trigger-key-positions, set to the opposite half plus the thumb cluster on every sunaku mod. Rolling A → O inside the left hand can never produce a layer; reaching A → H across the board always can. On top of it, require-prior-idle-ms refuses to start a hold that follows too close behind the previous keystroke — mid-word, the mods are simply switched off.

left handright hand
tapping term270 ms (230 on I)200 ms
quick tap300 ms300 ms (200 on S)
prior idle70 ms70 ms
guardright half + thumbsleft half + thumbs

The right hand is tuned 70 ms faster than the left throughout — I is the one exception, trimmed to 230 ms because it holds the symbols typed most — and the same numbers carry down to the bottom-row holds on X, J, V and the period key.

The right home row is the exception and stays on ⌘. Caps show the base legend, the line underneath shows the chord sent.

Where these sit in the stack

Layer numbers are positional in adv360.keymap — the order the nodes appear is the order ZMK indexes them — so removing a layer renumbers everything below it. Two layers came out recently, which moved Hyper from 14 to 12 and TextNav from 15 to 13.

#node
0sunaku_basethe default base — drawn above
1 · 2urob_base · tharshi_basethe other two bases
3 · 4cursor · fn_media
5 · 6sym · numsymbols, numpad
7cmd_arw_nav_right_handcommand right — hold space
8cmd_arw_nav_left_handcommand left — hold backspace
9 · 10text_nav_urob · visual_navigation
11systemreset, USB ⇄ BLE output
12hyperthe ⌃⌥⇧⌘ namespace
13text_navthe vim layer
14simplea base with no hold-taps at all

From layer 0 you can reach three of the bases directly: the outer top-left key goes to Cursor, the outer bottom-left key goes to Urob, and the inner top-left key sticks Tharshi for one keypress.
